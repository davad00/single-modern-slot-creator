## Findings

### 1. Market snapshot 2024–2026

- [observed] Online slots remain the dominant iGaming vertical: UK online gambling revenue reached £7.7bn in 2025 with slots contributing £5.4bn, up 15% YoY per UKGC figures cited in industry trend reporting [S20][S24]. LatAm is the fastest-growing region (Argentina $1.57bn market, 61% slot preference; Peru >$345m) [S24].
- [observed] The 2024–2026 release meta is dominated by: (a) very-high-volatility "streamable" games with 25,000x–500,000x max wins, (b) sequels/franchise extensions (Mental 2, Chaos Crew 3, Money Train 4, Iron Bank 2, Snake Arena 2, Big Bass Splash 1000, Holy Hand Grenade 2), and (c) "juiced" re-releases of mainstream franchises (Pragmatic's 1000-series and Super Scatter series) [S1][S2].
- [observed] Player-selectable risk is an emerging 2025 innovation: Relax's Iron Bank 2 ships three selectable bonus risk profiles; ELK's X-iter system offers 5 paid entry tiers as a more granular alternative to a single bonus buy [S1][S15].
- [observed] Session formats are shortening; industry reporting attributes collapsing dead-spin tolerance to streamer/TikTok clip culture (average session length reported down from ~45 to ~12 minutes) [S28][S30]. Short-session adjacent formats (crash, mines, plinko) are growing but are out of scope for this skill [S24].

### 3. Hit-game math profile norms (what commercially successful 2024–26 slots share)

- [observed] Default RTP ~96.0–96.5% across all major studios; operators can usually select certified down-tiers (94 / 92 / 90 / 88 clusters). ELK is the outlier shipping fixed 94–95% [S15][S33][S34]. A visible 2025–26 controversy: UK operators quietly deploying ~92–94% versions while marketing sheets cite ~96% [S34][S35].
- [mandatory, GB/MT/SE] Regulated markets require displaying the active RTP of the deployed version in-game [S33].
- [observed] Hit frequency: mainstream titles 20–33% (Money Train 4 = 20.14%, Fat Panda Happy Nets = 31.45%, WDOAW ≈ 19%); extreme-volatility outliers go below 10% (Tombstone Slaughter 9.39%) [S13][S26][S31][S1].
- [observed] Natural feature trigger rates cluster around 1/150–1/400 spins (Money Train 4: 1/387.4; Slot Tracker's Pragmatic aggregate: ~0.1% per spin) [S13][S23].
- [observed] Max-win norms by segment: mainstream/casual 5,000x–10,000x; streamer-facing high-vol 25,000x–50,000x; extreme "record chase" tier 100,000x–500,000x (Tombstone Slaughter 500,000x is the 2025 non-jackpot record; Money Train 4 150,000x at 1-in-94m probability) [S1][S13][S22].
- [observed] Bonus-buy pricing: 100x total bet is the industry anchor (established by BTG's White Rabbit, 2017); observed range 50x–100x standard, 80x for low-vol modes (WDOAW Train Robbery), 200x–500x for enhanced modes, up to 2,000x for extreme tiers (San Quentin Lockdown) [S36][S37][S31].
- [observed] Buy-mode RTP is typically ≥ base RTP by 0.2–0.5pp (Money Train 4: 96.10% → 96.50% buy; Razor Returns feature buy 96.78%) — a deliberate incentive design [S13][S16].
- [observed] Stake modifiers ("Push Bet", "Scatter Boost", Pragmatic "Ante Bet" +25%) that raise trigger frequency for +10–25% stake are now common alongside buys and survive in some markets where buys are banned — but see legal-review items [S16][S25].
- [observed] Grid formats in current hits: 6×5 pay-anywhere scatter (Pragmatic engine), 5×5 + few fixed lines (Hacksaw), 5×5 cluster (Print, Push), 5×3/5×4 ways or lines (classic), Megaways 6-reel 2–7 (licensed), expanding rows/ways (Razor Ways 729→46,656; Blitzways), compact 3×3 novelty (Fat Panda), top-modifier-reel hybrids [S1][S16][S21][S26].
- [observed] Multiple *materially distinct* bonus modes per game is a defining Hacksaw-era pattern (WDOAW's Duel at Dawn / Great Train Robbery / Dead Man's Hand each with own volatility and buy price) — this validates the skill's mandatory feature/super_feature/ultimate_feature tier separation [S31][S32].

### 4. Theme & art-style trends 2024–2026

- [observed] Evergreen themes with continuous 2024–26 hit output: Greek/ancient mythology (Gates/Olympus franchise), candy/fruit-modern (Sweet Bonanza, Sugar Rush), fishing (Big Bass, 20+ sequels), Western/outlaw (WDOAW, Le Cowboy, Tombstone, Dead Men Walking — the single most overrepresented streamer-meta theme), Asian fortune (Fat Panda launch titles), Egyptian "Book" adventure [S1][S21][S26][S38].
- [observed] Growth themes: dark fantasy / cosmic horror as a commercial category (deep blues/purples/poison greens differentiating in bright lobbies); horror/asylum/prison transgressive (Nolimit's lane — Mental 2, Dead Men Walking with sepia execution-square art); sci-fi/space and AI narratives; retro/early-2000s pixel nostalgia synthesized with modern animation [S38][S39][S24].
- [observed] Art-style trends: 2.5D painterly (hand-painted textures on 3D models, Hades-like), cartoon-exaggerated with bold silhouettes, and dark gritty desaturated 2D (Hacksaw's melancholic-painted identity; Nolimit's grim atmospheric identity). Cinematic quality ≠ photorealism: stylized + strong lighting, particle FX, slow-mo win moments, camera pans; Blender/UE5-class pipelines are now standard for hero assets [S38][S39].
- [recommended] Distinct visual identity is a competitive requirement in 2025+; derivative visuals are explicitly called out as commercially non-viable by industry art analyses [S38].
- [mandatory-adjacent, GB et al.] Themes must not be "primarily directed toward minors": candy/cartoon aesthetics are commercially proven but sit close to regulator concerns about appeal to under-18s (UK CAP code prohibits gambling content of "strong appeal" to children). Use adult framing, no child characters, no juvenile IP pastiche. [inferred from advertising-standards regime; flag for compliance dossier cross-check]

### 5. Streaming / content-creator influence on design

- [observed] Slots & Casino is a top-2 Kick category (~45m hours watched in June 2025; casino+IRL ~700m hours on Kick H1 2025 vs 400m on Twitch, where gambling remains restricted). Top channels: Trainwreckstv, Roshtein, ClassyBeef et al. [S40][S41].
- [observed] Games are now explicitly designed for streamability: big bonus-entry animations, extreme max wins as marketing numbers, bonus-buy-first session formats, dramatic "one-symbol-away" moments. The dominant stream loop is bulk bonus buying ("bonus hunt" openings) rather than base-game grinding [S28][S30][S42].
- [observed] Max-win chasing is a codified meta: streamer max-win records (e.g., $37.5m single-win headlines in 2025) drive game selection; providers publish max-win probability (e.g., 1/94m for Money Train 4) as a feature [S13][S30][S42].
- [observed] Credibility problems shadow the meta: fake-balance/test-account allegations against major streamers, Bloomberg reporting on statistically anomalous sponsored wins, and Kick removing hourly payouts for the category (Mar 2025) while restricting to age-verified sites (Feb 2025) [S30][S40].
- [recommended] Design consequence: a modern release needs (a) a headline max-win number ≥10,000x, (b) a visually spectacular, clip-friendly bonus-entry and max-win moment, (c) bonus-buy tiers where legal — while never using deceptive presentation (the skill's rule 9.5) [inferred from S1][S28][S30].

### 6. IP risk register — trademarked / licensed mechanics (NAME protection)

Key legal frame [observed]: mechanic *ideas* (cascades, ways, hold-and-respin, cluster wins) are broadly unprotectable as such once any patents lapse; what is protected is (1) trademarked mechanic NAMES and logos, (2) trade dress, (3) patents where they exist (rare, jurisdiction-specific), (4) trade secrets (math models, reel strips, par sheets), (5) copyright in art/audio/code/characters [S43][S44][S12].

### 7. IP risk register — patents & trade secrets

- [observed] **Telnaes virtual-reel patent (US 4,448,419, issued 1984, assigned to IGT 1988, EXPIRED 2002-02-24)**: the canonical slot patent precedent. It covered RNG-selected stops on a virtual reel larger than the physical reel; IGT licensed it industry-wide (Bally paid >$2m royalties) and won willful-infringement against WMS (Fed. Cir. 1999; ~$28.67m settlement). Lesson: slot-math patents CAN be enforced hard while alive; virtual-reel weighting itself is now public domain [S43][S44][S47].
- [observed] **BTG holds a US patent covering its Megaways mechanic** (referenced in its licensing announcements: "its U.S patented mechanic and MegaWays trademark") — the strongest currently-active mechanic patent claim in the online space. Exact patent number/claims not confirmed in this research pass — legal review required before shipping any 6-reel variable-symbols-per-reel game into the US [S4]. [inferred: outside the US the protection is primarily trademark, given the breadth of unlicensed "ways-variant" games in EU markets]
- [observed] **Aristocrat v. Light & Wonder (Dragon Train, filed Feb 2024 → settled Jan 2026 for $127.5m)**: primarily a TRADE-SECRETS case, not patents — L&W admitted using Aristocrat math models brought over by ex-employees, pulled Dragon Train/Jewel of the Dragon from market, and a Nevada injunction issued Sep 2024. Industry takeaway repeatedly stated in coverage: patents in slots are narrow and rarely decisive, but math models, par sheets, and design docs are protectable trade secrets — copying a competitor's math profile numerically is actionable even without patents [S45][S48][S49].
- [observed] Hold-and-respin as a mechanic (Lightning Link, 2014, designer Scott Olive) has been "widely imitated" without reported patent suits — protection has come via trade secrets and branding, not patents [S45][S46].
- [inferred] No blocking patent was found for: cascading/tumbling reels, scatter-pays-anywhere, cluster wins, expanding grids, persistent collection meters, stepping wild multipliers, symbol splitting, or bonus-buy UX. Given how many studios ship them unlicensed across regulated markets, they are safe as concepts under original names. Confidence high; still route through the compliance-review template's IP checklist.
- [recommended] Treat generated math as clean-room: the skill must never ingest or reproduce a competitor's reel strips, symbol weights, or paytable values; similarity should only exist at the level of public aggregate stats (RTP, volatility class, hit frequency, max win) [derived from S48].

### 8. Regulatory pressure points that shape market patterns (cross-reference dossiers 13–16)

- [mandatory, GB] Bonus buys are prohibited for GB players — UKGC position under the Remote Technical Standards, widely dated to 2019 (one source says 2023; exact RTS clause needs verification — see Uncertainties). Games ship with the buy button removed for UK [S36][S37].
- [mandatory, NL] Netherlands (KSA) also disallows bonus-buy features [S36].
- [observed, MT] MGA markets permit bonus buys — the feature's commercial home turf [S36].
- [mandatory, GB] Stake limits from April/May 2025: £5/spin (25+), £2/spin (18–24) [S20].
- [mandatory, GB] Slot design rules (effective 31 Oct 2021): ≥2.5s average spin cycle, no turbo/quick-spin or slam-stop, no autoplay, no celebration of wins ≤ stake (LDWs), display net position and session time. [observed via S20/S37 summaries; primary RTS citation to be pinned in the compliance dossier]
- [observed] Multi-RTP shipping (96/94/92/90) is the norm and is itself becoming a reputational issue (UK "RTP downgrade" coverage 2025–26) — the skill's 0.96 default with optional certified down-profiles matches market practice [S33][S34][S35].

## Design implications for the Skill

**Math defaults (confirm CONVENTIONS §11 against market):**
1. Target RTP 0.9600 default with optional certified profiles at 0.9400 / 0.9200 — matches market norm exactly [S33]. Always emit per-profile sim reports; always surface the active RTP for in-game display (GB/MT/SE mandate) [S33].
2. Max win default 10,000x is the correct mainstream anchor; allow `streamer_high` preset 25,000x–50,000x only when the math model proves the tail (record probability of max win, e.g. 1-in-tens-of-millions is acceptable and normal to publish) [S1][S13]. Never exceed 50,000x by default — 100k+ is a specialist record-chasing tier with brutal hit-frequency tradeoffs [S1].
3. Hit frequency: 22–30% for mainstream presets; never below 15% unless the brief explicitly asks for "extreme volatility" (market floor observed ~9.4%) [S13][S26][S1].
4. Natural `feature` trigger 1/180–1/280 spins (market cluster 1/150–1/400); `super_feature` 3–6× rarer; `ultimate_feature` 10–25× rarer than `feature` [S13][S23].
5. Bonus-buy pricing: `feature` = 100x (anchor), low-volatility alternate mode 60–80x, `super_feature` 200–400x, `ultimate_feature` 500–2,000x. Buy RTP may exceed base RTP by ≤ 0.5pp and MUST be separately simulated and disclosed [S13][S16][S31][S36].
6. Every game ships ≥2 materially distinct bonus modes (validates tier system); per-tier volatility and RTP must be documented per-mode like Hacksaw does (players/streamers expect per-bonus stats) [S31][S32].
7. Optional stake modifier ("boosted bet", +10–25% stake for ~2× trigger frequency) is a market-proven engagement lever — implement as a jurisdiction-gated flag, disabled when UNKNOWN jurisdiction [S16][S25].
